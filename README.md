# Clinic-Visit-Trends-and-Doctors-Performance-Overview


---

##  Overview

This project analyzes patient visit data to uncover insights into healthcare service patterns, treatment outcomes, and satisfaction levels.  
The goal is to transform raw patient data into a structured, visual, and decision-driven report that highlights how demographic and clinical variables influence satisfaction and treatment trends.

By leveraging Excel’s full analytical capacity — **Power Query, Power Pivot, Pivot Tables, DAX formulas, and interactive dashboards** — this analysis provides actionable insights that can guide hospital administrators, medical practitioners, and healthcare decision-makers in improving patient experiences.

---

##  Rationale for the Project

Healthcare organizations handle vast volumes of data daily, but without structured analysis, key insights remain hidden.  
This project was initiated to demonstrate how **data-driven approaches can enhance patient-centered care** by identifying patterns in treatment outcomes, satisfaction ratings, and demographic characteristics.

**Motivation:**
- Simplify complex patient data into an interactive, insight-rich dashboard.  
- Demonstrate expert use of Excel for end-to-end analytics.  
- Support decision-making that improves hospital efficiency and patient satisfaction.

---

## Objectives

1. Analyze visit distribution across different doctors and days.  
2. Evaluate satisfaction levels across patient categories and treatments.  
3. Understand demographic trends through age-group segmentation.  
4. Identify relationships between patient condition, treatment type, and satisfaction.  
5. Present findings in an interactive dashboard for clear, data-backed decision-making.

---

## 🗂️ Data Description

The dataset, sourced from **Kaggle**, contains **50 patient records and 10 columns**:

- **Patient Name**  
- **Visit ID**  
- **Visit Day**  
- **Doctor Name**  
- **Age**  
- **Gender**  
- **Treatment**  
- **Condition**  
- **Satisfaction Rating**  
- **Age Group**

**Age Group Categorization:**
- *Young Adult:* ≤ 29 years  
- *Adult:* 30–45 years  
- *Middle Age:* 46–60 years  
- *Elderly:* ≥ 61 years  

This structure enabled detailed demographic analysis while ensuring interpretability and segmentation across patient groups.

##  Project Scope

This project focuses on **analyzing patient satisfaction and operational efficiency** based on visits, treatments, and doctor performance.  
It excludes clinical accuracy or medical outcomes beyond satisfaction ratings.

**Deliverables include:**
- Cleaned, modeled dataset  
- Fully interactive Excel dashboard  
- Action-oriented report for decision-makers

---

##  Methodology

1. **Data Cleaning (Power Query):**  
   Removed duplicates, standardized treatment and condition names, corrected spelling errors, and categorized ages.

2. **Data Modeling (Power Pivot):**  
   Created relationships between demographics, visit details, and satisfaction ratings.

3. **Analysis (Pivot Tables):**  
   Summarized visits per doctor, satisfaction per treatment, and patient demographics.

4. **Visualization (Pivot Charts + Slicers):**  
   Designed dynamic visuals linked with slicers for interactivity by doctor, age group, and visit day.

5. **Automation (VBA Integration):**  
   Implemented a Reset Button to clear all filters — improving usability and presentation flow.

## 📈 Results (Key Findings)

1. **Doctor Performance:**  
   **Dr. Funmi** and **Dr. Grace** achieved the highest satisfaction ratings, reflecting outstanding patient experience delivery.

2. **Treatment Effectiveness:**  
   Preventive and follow-up treatments scored the highest satisfaction — proactive care leads to better perceptions.

3. **Age Influence:**  
   Adults (30–45 years) had the most frequent visits, suggesting strong healthcare engagement in this demographic.

4. **Visit Day Trends:**  
   Mid-week visits dominate, showing patient preference for midweek consultations and reduced weekend visits.

5. **Condition Insights:**  
   Patients with mild conditions were more satisfied, suggesting improvements in waiting time and care for severe cases could boost overall satisfaction.
